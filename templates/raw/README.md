# Cardiology Workflow Starter Template

This template is a practical, non-diagnostic workflow aid for outpatient cardiology visits. It is meant to standardize intake, documentation, follow-up, and safety escalation; it is **not** a substitute for clinician judgment, local protocols, or current professional guidelines.

## Intended use

Use this workflow to support:

- New patient or follow-up cardiology clinic visits.
- Pre-visit chart preparation.
- Rooming and symptom triage.
- Clinician documentation.
- Closing the loop on tests, referrals, and patient instructions.

## Safety first: escalation triggers

If any of the following are present, pause routine workflow and follow your site's urgent evaluation protocol:

- Active chest pain, chest pressure, or anginal equivalent symptoms that are severe, new, worsening, prolonged, or occurring at rest.
- Syncope or near-syncope with exertion, palpitations, chest pain, dyspnea, hypotension, or injury.
- New focal neurologic deficits, suspected stroke/TIA symptoms, or acute altered mental status.
- Severe dyspnea, hypoxia, pulmonary edema symptoms, cyanosis, or respiratory distress.
- Hemodynamic instability, sustained tachyarrhythmia/bradyarrhythmia, or concerning ECG findings.
- New unilateral leg swelling with dyspnea/chest pain, concern for pulmonary embolism, or other high-risk thromboembolic presentation.

For patient-facing instructions, use plain language such as: **"If you have severe or worsening chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, stroke-like symptoms, or feel you may be having an emergency, call 911 or seek emergency care now."**

## Pre-visit chart preparation

Complete before the appointment when possible.

### 1. Identify visit type and primary question

- [ ] New consult
- [ ] Follow-up
- [ ] Pre-operative cardiovascular evaluation
- [ ] Post-hospital follow-up
- [ ] Device follow-up
- [ ] Test-result review
- [ ] Other: `__________`

Primary clinical question: `__________`

### 2. Review recent events

- [ ] Emergency department visits or hospitalizations since last visit.
- [ ] Procedure notes, discharge summaries, and medication changes.
- [ ] Outside cardiology records and prior test reports.
- [ ] Relevant non-cardiology notes: primary care, nephrology, endocrinology, pulmonary, sleep medicine.

### 3. Pull objective data

- [ ] Vitals trend: blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, weight/BMI.
- [ ] Labs: lipids, A1c/glucose, CMP/renal function, CBC, TSH when relevant, BNP/NT-proBNP when relevant.
- [ ] ECGs and rhythm monitoring.
- [ ] Echocardiogram, stress testing, cardiac CT/MRI, catheterization reports.
- [ ] Device interrogation reports if applicable.

### 4. Medication reconciliation prep

- [ ] Compare medication list against discharge paperwork, pharmacy fill history, and patient-reported use.
- [ ] Flag high-risk discrepancies: anticoagulants, antiplatelets, antiarrhythmics, beta blockers, diuretics, RAAS inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, nitrates.
- [ ] Identify refill needs, adherence barriers, cost issues, and adverse effects.

## Rooming workflow

### 1. Structured symptom check

Ask and document interval change in:

- Chest pain/pressure or exertional symptoms.
- Dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea.
- Edema, weight gain, abdominal bloating.
- Palpitations, tachycardia episodes, irregular pulse notifications.
- Dizziness, presyncope, syncope, falls.
- Exercise tolerance and functional capacity.
- Bleeding, bruising, melena, hematuria, or anticoagulant complications.

### 2. Vital signs and measurements

- [ ] Blood pressure with appropriate cuff size; repeat if unexpectedly high/low.
- [ ] Heart rate and rhythm regularity.
- [ ] Oxygen saturation if dyspnea, pulmonary disease, heart failure, or cyanosis concern.
- [ ] Weight compared with baseline/dry weight when relevant.
- [ ] Orthostatic vitals when dizziness, syncope, falls, autonomic symptoms, or medication changes are relevant.

### 3. Patient goals and barriers

- [ ] Patient's top concern today: `__________`
- [ ] Medication access/cost barriers.
- [ ] Health literacy or language needs.
- [ ] Transportation, caregiving, food insecurity, or home monitoring barriers.
- [ ] Home BP/HR/weight log available: yes/no.

## Clinician assessment structure

Use a problem-based format.

### Problem: `__________`

- **Status:** stable / improving / worsening / new / uncontrolled
- **Key evidence:** symptoms, vitals, exam, ECG, labs, imaging, monitoring
- **Risk context:** age, comorbidities, prior events, family history, smoking/nicotine exposure, diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy/postpartum status when relevant
- **Assessment:** concise clinical interpretation
- **Plan:** diagnostics, therapy, monitoring, follow-up, education, contingency plan

## Prevention and risk-factor checklist

Use shared decision-making and current guideline-directed management. The American Heart Association's Life's Essential 8 framework highlights diet, physical activity, nicotine exposure, sleep, weight, blood lipids, blood glucose, and blood pressure as core cardiovascular health domains.

- [ ] Blood pressure: confirm measurement quality, home readings, adherence, side effects.
- [ ] Lipids/ASCVD risk: review recent lipids and risk discussion when preventive therapy is considered.
- [ ] Diabetes/glucose: review A1c/glucose status and cardiometabolic therapies when relevant.
- [ ] Nicotine: screen current use, secondhand exposure, vaping, and cessation readiness.
- [ ] Physical activity: document baseline, symptoms with exertion, and safe activity plan.
- [ ] Nutrition: sodium, saturated fat, ultra-processed foods, alcohol pattern, culturally appropriate changes.
- [ ] Weight: trend, patient goals, sleep apnea symptoms, metabolic risk.
- [ ] Sleep: duration, quality, snoring, witnessed apnea, daytime sleepiness.

## Testing and follow-up closure

Before the patient leaves:

- [ ] Confirm each ordered test has a clinical question and urgency.
- [ ] Explain what result types should trigger urgent contact.
- [ ] Verify preferred contact method and backup contact.
- [ ] Schedule follow-up interval before checkout when possible.
- [ ] Assign responsibility for result review and patient notification.
- [ ] Provide written medication changes with start/stop/continue instructions.
- [ ] Document emergency precautions and routine call-back instructions.

## Results management queue

Use this mini-SBAR for abnormal results.

- **Situation:** result and why it matters
- **Background:** relevant diagnosis, symptoms, medications, baseline comparison
- **Assessment:** likely significance and risk level
- **Recommendation:** patient contact, medication adjustment, additional test, urgent evaluation, follow-up date

Result owner: `__________`

Patient notified: yes/no/date/method

Next action due: `__________`

## Patient after-visit summary checklist

Include:

- [ ] Diagnoses or visit focus in plain language.
- [ ] Medication changes: what changed, why, and when to start.
- [ ] Home monitoring instructions: BP/HR/weight/symptoms, including when to call.
- [ ] Test plan: what is ordered, why, and scheduling instructions.
- [ ] Follow-up date or target interval.
- [ ] Emergency precautions.

## Quality improvement metrics

Track a small set weekly or monthly:

- Time from abnormal result to patient notification.
- Percentage of visits with completed medication reconciliation.
- Percentage of patients with documented home BP/weight plan when clinically relevant.
- Open test orders older than expected completion date.
- No-show rate for high-risk follow-up visits.
- Percentage of notes closed within local target time.

## Source anchors

This template intentionally avoids medication dosing or diagnosis-specific algorithms. For clinical decision-making, use current local protocols and professional guidance, including:
